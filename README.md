# Abstract

When patients with Parkinson's disease lose their ability to take care of themselves, and carers are no longer able to care for them at home, they will need long-term care. The risk factors for being admitted to a nursing home are unclear and there are few published studies. This work will identify the incidence of institutionalization by Kaplan-Meier estimate. And identify the risk factors for institutionalization using cox regression, using data from a prospective incident cohort of parkinsonism in North East Scotland.

# Introduction

Parkinson’s disease (PD) and atypical parkinsonism are neurodegenerative disorders. As the disease progresses, the needs for care in PD patients will increase and many patients will need to enter a long-term care institution, such as a nursing home. No previous studies have reported the incidence of institutionalisation in PD and atypical parkinsonism in a population-representative cohort. Several studies have investigated risk factors for institutionalisation. A previous study suggested that older patients who live alone with motor disability are tend to have higher probability entering the nursing home (Aarsland et al., 2000). And Safarpour et al. (2015) pointed out that the probability of being admitted into nursing home was associated with race and sex. In previous study, the researchers found that dementia, hallucinations cognitive decline, and hip fracture were risk factors for PD patients(Goetz and Stebbins, 1993; Aarsland et al. , 2000; Safarpour et al., 2015). Based on the previous studies’ finding, the clinicians can plan management earlier and provide more appropriate services to help PD patients. No previous studies have investigated risk factors in a population-representative cohort. No previous study has examined the influence of socioeconomic status on institutionalisation in parkinsonism.
